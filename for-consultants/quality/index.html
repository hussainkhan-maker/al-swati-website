Quality: Inspections, Hold Points, Records

We build to the approved documents and ask for inspection before work is covered. This page lists the stages, what is recorded and what happens when something does not conform. Items marked on request are arranged when the project asks for them.

1.0Principle

Build to the Approved Documents

  • Work starts from approved shop drawings and approved material submittals.
  • Installation is by our own team with a site supervisor.
  • No substitution without approval: the stone source, the fixing type, the bracket material and the anchor product stay as approved unless the engineer or consultant approves a change in writing.
  • What will be hidden is inspected before it is hidden: anchors and substructure before stone.

3.0Records

What Is Kept

Records kept where the project's inspection and test plan asks for them:

  • Approved shop drawings and material approvals.
  • Material and work inspection requests, with the consultant's comments.
  • Pull-out test reports, when tests are done.
  • For undercut anchors, the hole checks and tightening records that the anchor manufacturer's instructions require.
  • The facade engineer's site visit reports, on Route B projects.
  • Non-conformance reports and their close-out.
  • Photographs of anchors and substructure before the stone covers them.
4.0When it goes wrong

Non-Conformance

  1. Stop

    Work on the affected area stops when the supervisor, the engineer or the consultant finds something that does not match the approved documents.

  2. Record

    A non-conformance report states what was found, where, and on which panels or anchors.

  3. Propose

    The correction is proposed in writing. Where it changes a material, a fixing or a detail, it goes to the engineer or consultant for approval.

  4. Correct and release

    The correction is carried out, inspected and signed before the area is released.

5.0Tests and mock-up

Arranged on Request

Anchor pull-out tests

On request, pull-out tests on site are arranged with the anchor manufacturer or a testing laboratory, and witnessed by the consultant. The number of tests and the proof load are set by the engineer and the project specification.

For reference, published UK practice to BS 8539 tests at least 2.5% of the anchors, and at least three in each area, at 1.5 times the design action; a failure increases the sample [1]. A test report is evidence for the engineer, not an approval in itself [2].

Mock-up

On request, a mock-up is built from the approved materials and drawings by the same crew, inspected before the fixings are covered, and kept as the quality benchmark once approved. Guide specifications commonly require one before the work proceeds [3][4].

Material submittals and samples

Submitted for approval: stone samples that show the range, and the data for fixings, brackets and anchors.

6.0Scope

Scope of This Page

A test or a mock-up is recorded as done only where the project asked for it. The design, the acceptance criteria and the tolerances belong to the project engineer or consultant.
