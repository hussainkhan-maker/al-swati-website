2.0Undercut anchors

What the Two Assessed Anchors Have in Common

  • The hole. Drilled in the back of the panel with the maker's machine, cylindrical first and then widened at the base; every hole is checked with the maker's gauge [1][5].
  • The layout. Hilti's data gives four anchors per panel in a rectangular layout, a largest panel of 3.0 m² and minimum edge distances [1]. The engineer sets the positions from the assessment.
  • The stone. Each assessment covers selected natural stones. A stone that is not listed needs tests to the assessment document before it is used [1].
  • The rail. A hanger on each anchor hooks over a horizontal rail, so each panel hangs on its own. The rails and brackets are part of the system design. The section and the back-of-panel view are on the typical details page.
  • Porcelain. Thin ceramic and stoneware panels use their own anchors and assessments, tied to the panel maker's system [14][15].
3.0An assessment

What an ETA Covers and What It Does Not

  • An ETA describes the anchor: its materials, the hole, the resistance in the stones that were tested, and the assumptions of use [3][7].
  • It does not approve a facade. The panel size, the wind load, the rail, the brackets and the anchors to the structure are the project engineer's calculation.
  • It does not cover every stone. Riyadh limestone is a trade name for stone from several quarries, so the actual source is tested unless the maker confirms it in writing.
  • It is not a certificate held by the installer. We can install these systems; we hold no manufacturer certification.
4.0For a project

What to Ask the Maker For

  • The current assessment with its annexes and the declaration of performance.
  • Written confirmation that the project stone is covered, or the test plan if it is not.
  • The system proposal: rails, brackets and anchors, with the static calculation for the project's wind loads.
  • The drilling machine, the gauge and operator training.
  • Site pull-out tests and site support, where the specification asks for them.
  • For Rockany systems: the datasheet with the aluminium alloy and the stainless grade, and the project calculation.

In the guide specification, a maker's engineered system is Option B and assessed undercut anchors are Option C. Our experience with undercut fixing is with Hilti anchors, on 30 mm natural stone and on 20 mm porcelain.

Hilti, fischer and Rockany are names of their owners. They are named here to say what we can install and where the published data comes from.
