2.0System anatomy

Section and Components

Section through a mechanically fixed stone facade. Select a component for its function and the related pages.

1

Stone panel

Facing panel. Each panel is carried by its own fixings.

Natural stone is typically 30 mm; marble and granite are often 20 mm.

  • Stone and finishes
  • Which thickness, which fixing
2

Open joint

A gap around every panel.

Each panel expands and contracts with the daily heat without pushing the next one.

  • Typical details
  • Mechanical or wet fixing
3

Stone bracket

Carries the weight of the panel and restrains it against wind.

Up-Down angle, pin (PEN) or kerf rail, in stainless or hot-dip galvanised steel according to the project specification.

  • Fixing systems compared
  • Five systems in section
4

Cavity

The air space behind the stone.

There is no mortar behind the stone, so no mortar efflorescence (white salt stains) on the face.

  • Mechanical or wet fixing
  • Maintenance and handover
5

Vertical channel

Steel channel spanning floor to floor. It carries the stone brackets.

Part of the steel substructure, installed to the engineer's design where the wall behind is hollow block or the stone stands off the wall.

  • The steel substructure
  • How we deliver
6

Anchor

Fixes the bracket or the substructure to the concrete.

Type and size follow the engineer's calculation and the consultant's specification. Pull-out tests are arranged on request.

  • Hilti, fischer and Rockany
  • Quality and tests
7

Concrete structure

Slab edges, beams and columns: where every load ends.

The weight of the stone and the wind on it must reach the reinforced concrete.

  • Where the load goes
  • Guide specification 04 42 00
8

Infill wall

The block wall between the slabs.

Hollow block and other infill walls are not counted on to carry the cladding.

  • Channel or frame

3.0Audience

Written for the People Who Specify and Approve

DesignArchitects and designers

You are choosing the stone, the panel sizes and the joint pattern, and need to know which fixing system and thickness make them buildable.

ReviewConsultants and facade engineers

You write or review the specification and the submittals, and want to see the process, the tests and the records before the work starts.

DeliveryMain contractors' technical offices

You prepare prequalification and submittal packages and need a stone subcontractor whose statements hold up when the consultant reads them.

AL-Swati Contracting is based in Riyadh. Our team's experience goes back to 1994. Our scope is the supply and installation of mechanically fixed natural stone (mainly Riyadh limestone), marble, granite and porcelain facades, and the installation of the steel substructure behind them to the engineer's design.

4.0Scope

What We Install, Scope and Limits

What we install

  • Systems: Up-Down angles, pin (PEN) fixing, C-Channel, kerf rail and undercut anchors.
  • Undercut experience: Hilti undercut anchors on 30 mm natural stone and on 20 mm porcelain, on an ongoing commercial project for a major Saudi main contractor.
  • Brackets and angles: stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel, according to the project specification.
  • Anchor and bolt sizes: per the engineer's calculation and the consultant's specification.
  • Thickness: natural stone typically 30 mm, thicker on demand; marble and granite often 20 mm. Exterior 20 mm panels on edge fixings need the engineer's check and stone tests, or an undercut system.
  • Brands we can install: Hilti, fischer, Rockany, and local or imported accessories.
  • Warranty: a 5-year written workmanship warranty attested by the Chamber of Commerce, issued by the contracting company.

Scope and limits

Design is by the project engineer or consultant: we install to approved shop drawings, issued to us or prepared by the project's facade engineer. Brand names say what we can install; we hold no manufacturer certification. Sizes, wind values and stone strengths belong to each project's calculation and tests.
