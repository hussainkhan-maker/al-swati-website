Guide only – to be edited by the specifier; project design by the project engineer. The document is general guidance. It is not a design and not an offer. Square brackets mark blanks and choices; notes to the specifier are in italics and are deleted before issue.

2.0Options

Three Fixing Options

OptionSystemEvidence it carriesTypical use
A · Economy / localPin or Up-Down brackets, optionally on vertical C-channels, with locally available accessoriesProject calculation by the facade engineer; fixing tests in the project stone; material certificates per batchStone of 30 mm and more on concrete or solid masonry
B · Engineered systemA proprietary rail and bracket system with the system supplier's designThe supplier's calculation, shop drawings and data sheets for the projectVentilated facades, weak infill walls, regular grids
C · Assessed undercut anchorsUndercut anchors holding a European Technical Assessment, on horizontal railsThe anchor's assessment and declaration of performance, with stone-specific resistance from the assessment or from tests [1][2]Thin hard stone, porcelain, large panels, replaceable panels

The specifier keeps one option per stone type and deletes the others. An assessment describes the anchor; whether it covers a particular stone is confirmed with the manufacturer, and stone not listed needs tests [3].

3.0Contents

What the Document Covers

Part 1 – General

Summary and related sections; references; definitions; performance requirements (delegated design by a qualified facade engineer, wind loads per the current SBC 301, dead load carried per panel, safety factors, deflection limits, movement, corrosion and separation of dissimilar metals, fire); submittals; quality assurance; pre-construction testing; delivery and storage; site conditions; warranty.

Part 2 – Products

Stone and porcelain panels; fixing system (Options A, B, C); anchors to the structure; steel substructure and its corrosion protection; accessories; fabrication; source quality control.

Part 3 – Execution

Examination; preparation; installation of anchors and substructure; installation of stone; joints; tolerances; field quality control with pull-out proof tests; cleaning and protection; schedule of stone types and fixing options.

The structure follows the three-part format used in published guide and project specifications [4][5], and the content draws on the stone industry guidance cited across this section [6][7][8][9].

4.0Blanks

Project Values You Fill In

  • Code editions, wind parameters and design pressures from the project engineer.
  • Stone type, source, finish and thickness for each stone.
  • Safety factors and the standard they come from.
  • Deflection limits for the substructure and the backup.
  • Design temperature range and movement joint positions.
  • Bracket material: stainless steel grade, or hot-dip galvanised steel, and where each is used.
  • Number and proof load of site pull-out tests.
  • Tolerances, mock-up size and location, and warranty terms.
