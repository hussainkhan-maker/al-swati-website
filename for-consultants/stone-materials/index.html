Stone and Panel Materials

The stones and panels we install on facades, with what a specifier usually needs first: what the material is, the thickness we typically install, the standard that classifies it and what to check before it is approved. Strength values come from tests on the actual source, so none are published here.

General guidance. Each project is designed by its engineer or consultant. Updated: November 2026

In 30 seconds
  • The stones and panels we install: Riyadh limestone, travertine, marble, granite and porcelain.
  • Typical thickness: natural stone 30 mm; marble and granite often 20 mm.
  • Strength comes from tests on the actual source, so ask for samples and tests before approval.

Local stone

Riyadh Limestone Is a Trade Name

There is no single data sheet for Riyadh limestone. Published figures for Riyadh carbonate rock differ widely: one academic study of hard limestone in Riyadh reports water absorption around 9% [8], while trade sources quote much lower values. That range runs across the classes of ASTM C568, which allow at most 12%, 7.5% and 3% absorption for low, medium and high density limestone [1].

So the class and the design strength of a facade's limestone come from current tests on the quarry source that will supply the project. This is why we publish no strength value, and why range samples and test reports are part of the material submittal.

Imported stone

Marble and Granite on Exteriors

  • Marble in the sun. Thin marble outdoors can bow and lose strength under repeated heating [9]. A European test exists for it (EN 16306) [10]. For exterior marble in Riyadh, ask the engineer whether it is required.
  • Soundness. ASTM C503 limits exterior marble to soundness Group A [3]. Veined and decorative marbles in other groups belong indoors or need the engineer's review.
  • Granite. Dense and strong. The fixing type still decides whether a 20 mm exterior panel is acceptable.
  • Supply. Most marble used in Saudi Arabia is imported [11]. Each imported stone needs the quarry's current test reports, and a petrographic check where the stone is unfamiliar [12].
Submittal

Samples and Tests

  • Range samples. Several samples that show the lightest, the darkest and the most veined stone that will be accepted, in the project finish.
  • Test reports. Absorption and density, compressive strength, flexural strength and the strength at the fixing. The list with ASTM and EN numbers is in the tests table.
  • Finish and thickness. Bending tests are done at the project thickness and finish, because some finishes remove thickness [6][7].
  • No substitution. Once approved, the stone source stays as approved unless the consultant approves a change in writing. See quality.
