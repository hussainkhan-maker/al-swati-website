Maintenance and Handover Guide

A mechanically fixed facade needs little maintenance, but not none. This guide is for the owner and the facility team; consultants can attach it to the handover file.

General guidance. Each project is designed by its engineer or consultant. Updated: November 2026

Handover

What the Handover File Contains

  • A description of the facade: stone or panel types, the fixing system and the drawing references.
  • The approved shop drawings, and the project records agreed for handover.
  • Test reports, where tests were done.
  • The 5-year written workmanship warranty attested by the Chamber of Commerce, issued by the contracting company.
  • This maintenance guide, with the location of any spare panels kept for the building.
Inspection

Look at the Facade Regularly

The project engineer sets the inspection interval. As general guidance, look at the facade once a year and after any storm or impact, and check for:

  • Cracked, chipped, displaced or loose panels.
  • Joints that have opened, closed or lost their sealant.
  • Rust-coloured or white stains running from a joint.
  • Blocked ventilation openings at the base and top of the cavity.
  • Anything fixed to the facade since handover: signs, lights, cables, cameras.

Report anything on this list to the building's engineer and to us before repairing it. A stain or a moved panel is information about what is happening behind the stone.

Cleaning

Clean Gently

  • Remove dust with clean water and a soft brush.
  • Where a cleaner is needed, use a neutral one. Do not use acids or salt-based cleaners on limestone or marble [1].
  • Test any cleaner on a small hidden area first [2].
  • Do not use high-pressure washing at close range.
  • Keep irrigation spray and water features off the facade.
Do not

What Not to Do

  • Do not drill into panels or fix anything to them (signs, lights, cables) without engineering approval.
  • Do not fill the ventilation gaps or open joints. Venting the cavity is part of how the facade works [3].
  • Do not replace a panel with a different stone, thickness or fixing without the engineer's approval.
  • Do not let trades lean ladders or scaffolding on unprotected panel edges.
Repairs

Replacing a Panel

How a panel is replaced depends on the system:

  • Undercut on rails: each panel hangs alone and can be taken off and replaced on its own.
  • Pin, kerf and Up-Down: the fixings sit in the joints and are shared with neighbours, so panels are released in sequence. This is work for the installer.

Sealant joints, where the facade has them, have a limited life and are replaced when they crack or lose adhesion. Contact us for any replacement during or after the warranty period.

Sources

Public sources used for the technical statements on this page. Standards are summarised in our own words; where a standard was read through a secondary report, the entry says so. The project engineer should check the current edition before using any value.
