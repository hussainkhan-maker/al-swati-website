Architectural Facade Systems in Riyadh: Dry-Fix Stone

How architectural facade systems in Riyadh work with dry-fix stone cladding: brackets, pins, rails and anchors, with practical advice for Saudi heat and sand.

اقرأ هذا المقال بالعربية

Architectural facade systems in Riyadh have changed a lot over the past decades. Many buildings now use natural stone, marble, granite or large-format porcelain on the outside, fixed with mechanical systems instead of being glued or set in mortar. This method is called dry-fix cladding, and it is the approach we use on facades and walls at AL-Swati Contracting, with experience in Riyadh since 1994.

This article explains how dry-fix stone cladding works, what the main parts of the system are, and what to think about before you choose one for a villa or a commercial building in Saudi conditions.

What "dry-fix" means

In a dry-fix system, the stone panel is not bonded to the wall with adhesive or mortar. Instead, it is held in place by metal brackets, pins or rails that are anchored to the structure behind. Small gaps are left between the panels.

The main benefit is that the cladding is supported by its own fixings. The weight of the stone is carried by the metal system, and the wall structure is not asked to hold each panel with glue alone. This is why the approach is common for taller buildings and for thicker natural stone.

The main mechanical fixing systems

There is no single system that suits every project. The right choice depends on the stone, the panel size, the building height and the design. The systems we install include:

  • Up-Down brackets: a common system where an angle bracket on the wall supports the panel from below, and a second bracket holds it at the top. It is often used for villa facades.
  • PEN pins: pins fixed into the edge of the panel that connect to the support, suitable for certain stone types and sizes.
  • C-Channel: vertical steel channels fixed to brackets on the wall; the stone brackets bolt to these channels. It is used on larger facades.
  • Kerf rails: panels are fixed into slots cut in their edges, held by a rail behind. This system is typically more demanding to install.
  • Undercut anchors: anchors that sit in a groove cut on the back of the panel, giving a hidden fixing.

Each system has its own cost and installation time. In general, C-Channel, kerf and undercut systems cost more per square metre than Up-Down brackets, because they require more material, more precise work and more labour.

We can install systems from Hilti, fischer and Rockany, as well as local or imported accessories. Brackets and angles are supplied in stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel, according to the project specification. Anchor and bolt sizes are set by the calculation of the project's facade engineer and the consultant's specification, not by guesswork on site.

Why Saudi conditions matter

Riyadh has very high summer temperatures, strong sun, dust and sand, and occasional strong winds. These conditions affect a facade in several ways.

Heat makes stone and metal expand and contract. Dry-fix systems allow for this movement through the way the panels are fixed and the gaps left between them. If the gaps are too small or the fixings are too rigid, the stress can show up as cracks or misalignment over time.

Sand and dust collect in joints and on surfaces. Choosing a stone with a suitable finish, and planning a simple cleaning routine, makes a visible difference after a few years.

Wind loads on tall buildings need to be considered in the design of the fixings. This is one reason why commercial towers often need safety scaffolding during installation, which adds cost to the work.

Panel thickness and material

Panel thickness affects both the look and the fixing. Natural stone is typically 30 mm thick, and it can be made thicker on demand. Marble and granite are often 20 mm. Thicker panels are heavier and need stronger fixings, so the thickness should be agreed with the project's facade engineer early in the design.

Before work starts, we submit material samples and submittals for approval. This allows the consultant and the client to check colour, finish and dimensions before any panels are cut.

Cost factors to know

Prices vary by stone type, system and building. As an example for a villa facade in Riyadh, supply and installation on Up-Down brackets is about 230 to 280 SAR per m² for Riyadh white stone, and about 265 to 340 SAR per m² for large-format porcelain. More complex systems cost more. For instance, Riyadh white stone with C-Channel is about 350 to 410 SAR per m². Commercial towers add roughly 20 to 25 SAR per m² for safety scaffolding. All these prices exclude 15% VAT, and shop drawings are quoted separately.

Frequently asked questions

What is a dry-fix stone cladding system?

It is a facade system where stone panels are held by mechanical brackets, pins, rails or anchors instead of adhesive or mortar. Panels are fixed with small gaps between them.

Which fixing system is best for a villa facade?

Up-Down brackets are a common choice for villa facades and cost less than C-Channel, kerf or undercut systems. The right system depends on the stone, panel size and design, so it should be confirmed with the project's facade engineer and the consultant.

How thick should stone cladding panels be?

Natural stone is typically 30 mm thick and can be made thicker on demand, while marble and granite are often 20 mm. Thickness should be agreed early with the project's facade engineer because thicker panels need stronger fixings.

Do you install stainless steel or galvanised brackets?

Yes. Brackets and angles are supplied in stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel according to the project specification, and anchor sizes follow the calculation of the project's facade engineer.
