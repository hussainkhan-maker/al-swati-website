Facade Stone in Riyadh: Types, Finishes and How It Is Fixed

Facade stone for villas and buildings in Riyadh: Riyadh stone, limestone, granite, marble and porcelain, where each suits, and how it is fixed mechanically.

اقرأ هذا المقال بالعربية

Choosing facade stone is the first decision an owner makes about how a building will look for decades. This guide goes through the stones most often used on facades in Riyadh, where each one suits, the finishes and layouts you can ask for, and how the stone is fixed to the building. AL-Swati Contracting brings 500+ projects of experience since 1994 in stone facades in Riyadh.

The main types of facade stone

Riyadh stone (local limestone)

Riyadh stone is the light, cream to white limestone that gives many of the city's villas and public buildings their colour. It suits large, calm wall surfaces, frames around windows and tall pilasters. It can be supplied smooth, or chiselled by hand for a rougher, more traditional look. See it on our projects, for example the white Riyadh stone and glass facade in Irqa.

Other limestones and travertine

Imported limestones and travertine give warmer beige and cream tones. Travertine has a clear grain and small natural holes, so the direction of the grain has to be planned: all panels vertical, or all horizontal. Our travertine facade in Irqa shows a vertical grain across two wings.

Granite

Granite is hard and dense, and it keeps its polish outdoors. That makes it a common choice for the base of a building, entrances and the lower floors, where the facade meets dust, water and people. It comes in greys, blacks, reds and browns.

Marble

Marble is chosen for its veining and its colour. Outdoors it is usually used on feature walls and entrances rather than on a whole facade, because the sun and sand can dull a polished surface over the years. A honed finish hides this better than a mirror polish. Read more on our marble installation page.

Porcelain panels

Large porcelain panels are not natural stone, but they are now common on modern facades. They are thin, light and uniform in colour, and they can imitate stone, concrete or metal. They need their own fixing system, chosen by the project's facade engineer.

Finishes and layouts

The same stone can look very different depending on its finish and on how the panels are laid out:

  • Finish: polished, honed (smooth and matt), bush-hammered, sandblasted, or hand-chiselled.
  • Panel shape: large rectangular panels for a modern look, narrow strips, or mixed heights for a traditional wall.
  • Joints: open or sealed, aligned in a grid or staggered like brickwork.
  • Details: window frames, cornices, columns and corner pieces cut from the same stone.

Ask for a sample panel of the real stone in the real finish, and look at it outside in daylight before you decide.

How facade stone is fixed

There are two ways to put stone on a wall. With cement or adhesive, the stone is glued to the wall. With mechanical fixing, each panel is carried by metal brackets fixed to the structure, with an air gap behind the stone. For facades in Riyadh's heat we install with mechanical fixing: the stone can move with the temperature, and a damaged panel can be taken off and replaced without breaking out mortar. The fixing design is approved by the project's facade engineer. The steps are explained on our stone cladding page.

Which stone for which building?

  • Villa: Riyadh stone or limestone for the main walls, with granite at the base and a marble or chiselled feature at the entrance.
  • Commercial building: stone frames and pilasters between glass, in white Riyadh stone or travertine.
  • Tower: lighter panels and a fixing system designed floor by floor by the project's facade engineer.

What does it cost?

The cost depends on the stone, the area, the height of the building and the fixing system. You can get a first figure in a few minutes with our stone cladding cost calculator, then send us your drawings for a written quotation.

Frequently asked questions

Which facade stone is most used in Riyadh?

Riyadh stone, the local light limestone, is the most familiar on villas and buildings in the city. Granite, marble, travertine and porcelain are used with it or instead of it, depending on the design.

Is facade stone fixed with cement?

It can be, but we install facades with mechanical fixing: each panel is carried by metal brackets fixed to the structure.

Is there a warranty?

Yes: a 5-year written workmanship warranty attested by the Chamber of Commerce, issued by the contracting company.
