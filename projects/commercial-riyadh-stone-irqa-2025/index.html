AL-Swati Contracting supplied and installed the stone facades of a commercial building in Irqa, Riyadh. The work covered about 1,500 m²: white Riyadh stone on the main facades, fixed mechanically to the structure, and chiselled (rock-face) Riyadh stone on the side tower and the ground-floor piers. Our own team completed the work in 2025.

Irqa is a district on the north-western edge of Riyadh. The building has commercial units on the ground floor under a long canopy, and three floors above. Its design is built around tall white stone fins that run the full height of the facade between strips of glazing, with a black perforated metal screen in the middle. You can see more of our work on our projects page.

What a commercial facade like this needs

A facade of tall fins is all edges. Each fin has a front face and two returns, so the stone has to be cut accurately, the corners have to be sharp, and the joints have to line up from the canopy to the top of the building. On a long street front, the colour of the stone also has to stay even from fin to fin, or the facade looks patched.

At street level the needs are different. The piers and the side tower are close to people, cars and deliveries, so the stone there has to take knocks and hide dust and wear. And on a building of this height, every panel has to be carried safely by the structure, not held by adhesive alone.

The solution: white Riyadh stone, with chiselled stone at street level

We used white Riyadh stone for the fins and the main walls. Its light colour suits the clean, modern lines of the design, and a light facade takes up less of the sun's heat than a dark one.

For the side tower and the ground-floor piers we used chiselled Riyadh stone, known in Arabic as manhoot. Its rough, chiselled face gives texture where people see the building up close, and it shows dust and small marks far less than a smooth face.

The white stone is fixed mechanically, so every panel is carried by fixings anchored into the building's structure. Read more about stone cladding in Riyadh and the fixing systems we use, or about our work in Irqa.

How we install facades like these

Our own direct workforce installs every facade; we do not subcontract. The work follows the same steps:

  1. Site survey and measurement of the facades, fins, openings and the canopy line.
  2. Fixing design, approved by the project's engineer before work starts.
  3. Setting out of lines and levels, so the fins stay straight and the joints line up from floor to floor.
  4. Anchors and brackets fixed into the structure.
  5. Stone panels, fin faces and returns, cut to size and hung on their fixings, with movement joints where the design needs them.
  6. Chiselled stone on the side tower and the ground-floor piers.
  7. Cleaning and inspection of every finished area, then handover.

Built for Riyadh's heat and dust

A mechanically fixed facade leaves a gap between the stone and the wall. Air moves behind the panels, which helps keep heat off the wall and lets moisture dry out. Movement joints let the facade move with Riyadh's hot days and cooler nights without cracking. The light colour of white Riyadh stone reflects much of the sun, and the rough face of chiselled stone hides dust between cleanings. After a sandstorm, the stone cleans with water, a mild cleaner and a soft brush; acid should never be used on limestone.

The finished building

The photos show the finished facades: the tall white stone fins and walls, the side tower in chiselled stone, the white stone around the glazing, and the chiselled stone piers along the ground floor. We tidied the photos for this page (vehicles, rubble and loose material removed, the canopy panels shown without their printed protective film, and construction dust and stains on the stone cleaned off); the building and the stonework are shown as built.

The work carries a 5-year written workmanship warranty attested by the Chamber of Commerce, issued by the contracting company.

Frequently asked questions

Is white Riyadh stone a good choice for commercial buildings?

Yes. White Riyadh stone is a local natural stone with a light, even colour that suits modern designs. A light facade takes up less heat than a dark one, and with mechanical fixing the stone suits tall elements like the fins on this building.

What is chiselled (manhoot) stone, and where is it used?

It is natural stone with a rough, chiselled face instead of a smooth one. It is often used at street level, on bases, piers and feature walls, because it adds texture and shows dust and small marks less than smooth stone.

Can you supply and install two stone finishes on one building?

Yes. On this building we supplied and installed both: white Riyadh stone on the main facades, and chiselled stone on the side tower and the ground-floor piers.
