AL-Swati Contracting supplied and installed the white Riyadh stone on the facade of a commercial building in Irqa, Riyadh. The stone frames the building's glass curtain walls: tall pilasters, the top band and a central feature panel, all fixed mechanically on Up-Down brackets by our own team. The work was completed in 2025.

This is our second project in Irqa, a district on the north-western edge of Riyadh; the first was the white stone and chiselled stone facade of another commercial building. Here the design is mostly glass: large dark glass curtain walls with black metal frames, divided by white stone pilasters and finished at the top with a white stone band under a dark coping. In the middle, a tall stone panel with two vertical grooves breaks up the glass. You can see more of our work on our projects page.

What a glass and stone facade like this needs

Stone next to glass shows every line. The pilasters have to be perfectly plumb from the ground to the roof, the top band has to be level, and the stone has to meet the black frames of the curtain walls with clean, even gaps. In the feature panel the joints are staggered and two vertical grooves run its full height, so each piece has to be cut and set exactly for the lines to stay straight.

Because the stone sits between large glass panels, it also has to be fixed so that it can be adjusted to the curtain wall frames, and so that it can move with the heat without cracking.

The solution: white Riyadh stone on Up-Down brackets

White Riyadh stone gives a light, even colour that stands out against the dark glass and the black frames. On Up-Down brackets, each stone is carried by Up-Down angles anchored into the structure: the groove in the bottom edge of the upper stone sits on the angle's upper tongue, and the top groove of the lower stone takes the lower tongue, with no mortar. The brackets can be adjusted during installation, so the stone faces line up with the curtain wall frames.

How we install facades like these

Our own direct workforce installs every facade; we do not subcontract. The work follows the same steps:

  1. Site survey and measurement of the pilasters, the top band, the feature panel and the openings around them.
  2. Fixing design, approved by the project's engineer before work starts.
  3. Setting out, so the pilasters are plumb, the top band is level and the stone lines up with the curtain wall frames.
  4. Up-Down brackets anchored into the structure.
  5. Stone pieces cut to size and hung on their brackets, with the staggered joints and the vertical grooves aligned, and movement joints where the design needs them.
  6. Cleaning and inspection of every finished area, then handover.

Built for Riyadh's heat and dust

A mechanically fixed facade leaves a gap between the stone and the wall. Air moves behind the stone, which helps keep heat off the wall and lets moisture dry out. Movement joints let the facade move with Riyadh's hot days and cooler nights without cracking. The light colour of white Riyadh stone reflects much of the sun. After a sandstorm, the stone cleans with water, a mild cleaner and a soft brush; acid should never be used on limestone.

The finished building

The photos show the finished facade: white stone pilasters and the top band framing the glass, the feature panel with its two vertical grooves, and the stone at the corners and around the covered parking entrance. We tidied the photos for this page (traffic cones, cars and loose items removed, and construction dust and drip marks cleaned off the glass and the stone); the building and the stonework are shown as built.

Frequently asked questions

Does white Riyadh stone go well with glass curtain walls?

Yes. Its light colour stands out against dark glass and black frames, and as pilasters, bands and feature panels it gives a glass building a clear, strong outline.

Why use Up-Down brackets for stone between glass?

Each stone is carried by Up-Down angles anchored into the structure, with no mortar. The brackets can be adjusted on site, so the stone lines up with the curtain wall frames, and movement joints let it move with the heat.

Can stone be installed on a facade that is mostly glass?

Yes. On this building the stone forms the pilasters, the top band and one feature panel between large curtain walls. Each piece is fixed to the structure, so the stone does not depend on the glass or its frames.
