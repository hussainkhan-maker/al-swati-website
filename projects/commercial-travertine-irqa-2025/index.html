AL-Swati Contracting supplied and installed the travertine marble facades of a commercial building in Irqa, Riyadh. Our own team clad the upper floors of both wings, the deep window reveals and the base of the building in travertine, fixed mechanically to the structure. The work was completed in 2025.

This is our third project in Irqa, a district on the north-western edge of Riyadh; see also our white stone and chiselled stone facade and our white stone and glass facade in the same district. This building has shops and restaurants on the ground floor under brown canopies, and three floors above. Its two wings are clad in travertine with a vertical grain, cut by tall windows set in deep stone-lined reveals and by dark brown bands at each floor. Between the wings, a glazed entrance block with a brown louvred fin marks the way in. You can see more of our work on our projects page.

What a travertine facade like this needs

Travertine is a natural stone with a strong grain and small natural holes, so the look of the facade depends on how the panels are laid out. On this design the grain runs vertically, so the panels have to follow one direction and keep an even colour across both wings.

The deep reveals around the windows mean many returns and inside corners, where the stone has to meet cleanly on every floor. And every panel, including the reveal linings, has to be carried by the structure, not held by adhesive alone.

The solution: travertine marble with mechanical fixing

Travertine gives a warm, natural cream colour that sits well with the dark brown bands, window frames and canopies. We supplied the travertine and installed it with mechanical fixing, so each panel hangs on fixings anchored into the building's structure, with an air gap behind it.

How we install facades like these

Our own direct workforce installs every facade; we do not subcontract. The work follows the same steps:

  1. Site survey and measurement of the facades, the window openings and their reveals.
  2. Fixing design, approved by the project's engineer before work starts.
  3. Setting out of lines and levels, so the joints and the grain run straight from floor to floor and meet the brown bands neatly.
  4. Anchors and brackets fixed into the structure.
  5. Travertine panels, reveal linings and returns, cut to size and hung on their fixings, with movement joints where the design needs them.
  6. Cleaning and inspection of every finished area, then handover.

Built for Riyadh's heat and dust

A mechanically fixed facade leaves a gap between the stone and the wall. Air moves behind the panels, which helps keep heat off the wall and lets moisture dry out. Movement joints let the facade move with Riyadh's hot days and cooler nights without cracking. Travertine's light, warm colour takes up less of the sun's heat than a dark stone, and its natural texture hides dust between cleanings. Clean it with water, a mild neutral cleaner and a soft brush; never use acid on travertine or marble.

Frequently asked questions

Is travertine marble a good choice for facades in Riyadh?

Yes. Travertine has a warm, light colour that takes up less of the sun's heat than dark stone, and with mechanical fixing and movement joints it handles Riyadh's hot days and cool nights. Its natural texture also hides dust well.

How do you clean a travertine facade?

With water, a mild neutral cleaner and a soft brush. Never use acid, because it etches travertine and marble.

Can travertine be used in deep window reveals?

Yes. On this building the reveals around the windows are lined with travertine. Each piece is cut to size and fixed to the structure, so the returns and corners meet cleanly.
