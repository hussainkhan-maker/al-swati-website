Mechanical Fixing vs Wet Cement Fixing

How the two methods hold up in Riyadh. Select a row to see why.

Mechanical fixingWet cement fixing
Mechanical fixing

Each panel hangs on its own anchors with a joint around it, so it expands and contracts freely with the daily heat.

Wet cement fixing

Stone, mortar and wall expand at different rates. Day-and-night heat cycles stress the mortar bond and can open hollow spots behind the stone.

Mechanical fixing

Our engineer calculates the wind load for each project to SBC 301 and sets anchor type and spacing for it.

Wet cement fixing

Holding power depends on full mortar contact behind every panel, which cannot be checked once the stone is set.

Mechanical fixing

No mortar behind the stone, so no cement salts wash out onto the face: no mortar efflorescence.

Wet cement fixing

Water moving through cement mortar carries salts to the face and leaves white efflorescence stains, most visible on light Riyadh limestone.

Mechanical fixing

Every panel is held by stainless-steel anchors, so none can fall from a failed mortar bond.

Wet cement fixing

If the mortar bond fails behind a panel, nothing else holds it, and hollow spots are not visible from outside.

Mechanical fixing

A damaged panel can be taken out without breaking out mortar; on C-Channel rails each panel is replaced on its own.

Wet cement fixing

Replacing a panel means chiselling out mortar, which risks cracking the panels around it.

FAQ

Questions from Consultants

What stone thickness do you use on mechanically fixed facades?

30 mm for limestone and granite and 20 mm for marble. Travertine and large-format porcelain thickness is set in the project design.

How is wind load designed?

Our engineer calculates the wind load for each project to the Saudi Building Code (SBC 301), and that sets the anchor type and spacing.

Which fixing systems do you install?

Up-Down angle brackets, PEN pin fixing, vertical C-Channel rails, kerf rails and undercut anchors, all with Hilti and Fischer certified anchors.

Are you registered and SASO compliant?

We are registered with the Saudi Contractors Authority (SCA), and all materials comply with SASO specifications.

Can we get CAD details and a method statement for our project?

Yes. Send the request form on this page with your company and contact details, and we reply within one business day.
