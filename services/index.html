Stone cladding in Riyadh: systems, process & FAQ

Mechanical Fixing Systems
01

Up-Down Bracket System

A versatile angle-bracket fixing method that supports stone panels from above and below, distributing load evenly across the facade. Ideal for large-format natural stone and porcelain on high-rise and commercial buildings.

02

PEN Fixing System

A pin-and-groove mechanical fixing system that conceals all hardware behind the stone face, delivering a seamless, joint-free facade appearance. Preferred for premium residential and hospitality projects.

03

C-Channel Rail System

Vertical channels fixed to the wall on brackets; the stone brackets bolt to the channels. Suited to large commercial facades, tall storeys and walls that cannot take brackets directly.

Undercut Anchor Systems
01

Undercut Anchor Fixing

Think of this like a hidden bolt inside the stone itself. A small hole is drilled into the back of the stone panel and a stainless-steel anchor expands inside it. The anchor carries a hidden bracket that hangs on horizontal aluminium rails, fixed to vertical aluminium supports on the wall, so no screws or brackets are visible from the outside. Our experience includes Hilti undercut anchors on 30 mm natural stone and on 20 mm porcelain. The result is a clean, seamless facade that looks like the stone is simply floating on the building.

02

Kerf Rail System

A narrow groove (kerf) is cut along the top and bottom edges of each stone panel, and the thin blade of a horizontal rail slides into it. The rails are fixed to vertical supports on wall brackets. The rails, brackets and anchors to the wall follow the engineer's calculation for each project. It's a simple, reliable method used for decades, especially for marble walls in building lobbies, hotel corridors, and feature walls where a tidy, hardware-free finish matters.
