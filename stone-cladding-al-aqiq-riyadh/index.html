Limestone for facades in Al Aqiq

Limestone is the stone we are installing in Al Aqiq now, and it suits the district well. Its warm beige and sand tones fit Riyadh's architecture, look calm in strong sun, and sit well next to glass and metal on modern commercial buildings.

  • Finishes: honed and brushed finishes give a soft, even look; textured finishes add depth on large walls and hide fine dust.
  • Thickness: with mechanical fixing we typically use natural stone panels 30 mm thick, thicker on demand. Marble and granite are often 20 mm; exterior 20 mm panels on edge fixings need the engineer's check and stone tests, or an undercut system.
  • Other materials: we also install natural Riyadh stone, granite, marble and large-format porcelain. See marble installation in Riyadh for interiors and entrances.

What to check in a sample: look at the colour range across several panels, not one; check the edges and corners for chips; ask for the finish you want on a real panel; and compare the sample with the stone that arrives on site before installation starts.

Facade standards for commercial buildings and villas in Al Aqiq

A stone facade in Al Aqiq is often on a tall commercial building, where the panels sit high above the street. That changes what the fixing has to do.

  • Codes and materials: wind loads are calculated for each project by the project engineer to the current SBC 301, and materials and samples are submitted for approval before work starts.
  • Engineer's approval: the fixing design is approved by the project's engineer before work starts.
  • Wind load: taller facades take more wind, so the brackets, anchors and their spacing are chosen for the building's height and exposure.
  • Mechanical fixings: every panel is carried by mechanical fixings anchored into the structure, never by adhesive alone. Brackets are stainless steel or hot-dip galvanised steel according to the project specification.
  • Movement joints: joints let the stone and the building move with heat without cracking.
  • Fixing systems: we install Up-Down brackets, PEN pins, C-Channel rails, kerf rails and undercut anchors, chosen to suit each building, and can install systems from Hilti, fischer and Rockany. On our Al Aqiq tower, limestone is fixed with Up-Down brackets, which support each panel from above and below and spread the load evenly.

How our facades handle Riyadh's 45°C summer heat and dust

Summer in Riyadh reaches about 45°C, and dust storms are part of the year. A mechanically fixed stone facade is built for that.

  • Thermal movement: stone and concrete expand in the heat and contract at night. Movement joints and adjustable brackets let the panels move a little instead of cracking.
  • Ventilated gap: mechanical fixing leaves a gap between the stone and the wall. Air moves behind the panels, which keeps heat off the wall and lets moisture dry out.
  • Colour in strong sun: natural limestone keeps its colour in sunlight, and light, warm tones absorb less heat than dark stone.
  • Water absorption: limestone is porous, so a breathable stone sealer helps keep out water and dirt.
  • Dust: honed or textured finishes in warm tones show dust less than dark, polished stone.
  • Cleaning after sandstorms: use water and a mild cleaner with a soft brush. Never use acid on limestone or marble, because it etches the surface.
